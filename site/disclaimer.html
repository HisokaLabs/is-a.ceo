Independent projects

A subdomain belongs to the person who registered it. The project behind it is theirs alone. {{org_name}} does not review, endorse, host, operate, or maintain it, and a name resolving under one of these zones says nothing about the quality, legality, or safety of what it points to.

No affiliation

Registration does not create a partnership, an agency, an employment relationship, or any endorsement. Do not present a subdomain as being operated by {{org_name}}, and do not use one to imply that a product is official, certified, or sponsored.

Not a domain you own

  • The zone is controlled by {{org_name}}. The subdomain is a delegation, revocable at any time.
  • You cannot transfer it, sell it, or take it to another registrar.
  • If the zone changes operator or is discontinued, the subdomain goes with it.
  • Search engines may treat the name as part of this domain, not as an independent site.

If the project matters, put it on a domain you own and treat the subdomain as a convenience alias. There is no migration path other than changing your DNS target.

Availability

No availability guarantee is made. Resolution depends on resolvers, on the registry automation running, and on the entry being valid. Records can stop resolving because of an upstream problem that has nothing to do with the project using them.

Content responsibility

Responsibility for content at a subdomain rests entirely with the person who registered it. Reports of abuse are handled by removing the record, which is the only lever a DNS operator has. See the abuse policy.

Trademarks

Names that match a registered trademark are refused when identified, and removed when reported by the holder. A merged entry is never a grant of trademark rights, and being first to a pull request does not create priority over a rights holder.
